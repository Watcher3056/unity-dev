# The verdict: criteria and the bar

Part of the unity-dev skill, read by the main thread, R&D branches, critics, the audience critic and the judge (SKILL.md, **Skill files by role**). The criteria every branch is written to and the critics break it against, the bar the judge scores every core by, and how the comparison ends; the stage itself is in [rnd.md](rnd.md).

## Criteria

- [RND-CRITERIA] **Always:**
  - what the product's user gets, where there is one: what they see, do and feel, and how the experience around the change holds (smoothness, response, readability, convenience, etc.);
  - solves the real goal (root cause, not symptom);
  - regression risk;
  - fit with existing pipelines and project standards (from the profile);
  - scope of change;
  - behavior outside the requested scenario: whether the variant changes it, and where (behavior per [RND-BEHAVIOR]; [SCOPE-TRIGGER] in SKILL.md); a "yes" leaves the choice to the user, even when they said to pick;
  - maintainability over time;
  - verifiability.
- **If applicable** (state why it applies or not):
  - per-frame CPU cost and allocations;
  - memory;
  - designer tunability;
  - networking or multiplayer;
  - platform and build impact;
  - etc.

## The bar

- [RND-BAR] **The verdict scores every core against one bar, row by row, with a verdict and what shows it in every cell.** Goal: no core wins on a row nobody scored, and the criteria above are answered in one form the main thread and the user read at a glance. The rows:
  1. **Effect,** in two cells scored apart ([RND-EFFECT] in [rnd.md](rnd.md)): **for the user,** where the task has a product's user, their result now → after as [RND-EFFECT] states it, and how the experience around it holds, with the audience critic's findings ([RND-AUDIENCE]), scored by what that prediction says, since before the change nothing is shown yet: ✅ it reaches what the request came for; ⚠️ it reaches it, but the experience around it gets worse (a jump, a wait, etc.), named; ❌ it doesn't, or only in part ([RND-EFFECT]); **in the task's measure,** the core reaches what the request came for, in its measure as the request's own words give it (a load that spikes for several frames, a wait, a count, a rate, etc.), not a threshold the user must set, as an estimate by counting from the materials ([FIND-GIVEN] in [findings.md](findings.md)). "Not measured" or "not checked" where the materials bound it is the judge's own miss; short of what the request came for in either cell is ❌.
  2. **Kept:** every kept item of the spec ([SPEC-KEEP]; an item whose status is an assumption doesn't count here, [RND-ASSUMED]) still holds in the requested scenario; a result of the chain the materials show and the spec doesn't list is scored here too and named as the spec's gap ([SPEC-LIVE] in [spec.md](spec.md)); a stop the design itself makes in that scenario is ❌ (scored here; [RND-ASSUMED] counts it against effect), a stop only under conditions not shown is ⚠️ with the risk named ([RND-ASSUMED]); the gap row stays within [RND-ASSUMED]'s bounds (not a coupling the change replaces, nor what the words ask for); a result kept in a lesser degree (some accuracy for speed, etc.) is named as a trade.
  3. **Place:** the change acts at the link where what the request came for sits (the direction sheet's link row, [RND-DIRS]); a change at another link is named as acting around it, with what that link still does wrong.
  4. **Way:** the project's own way for the same situation, a usual technique of the field, or a new mechanism (the sheet's analogue and usual rows); for a new one, why the known ways can't serve.
  5. **Guards:** every guard, fallback, extra lookup, extra check, retry or extra state answers a case shown in the project, by reading, in the editor, in the running product, etc., as [RND-RISK-COST] counts it ([DIR-STATE]); the ones that cost something in code and answer none are named to drop.
  6. **Footprint:** what else passes through the changed place, and what changes outside the requested scenario ([RND-BEHAVIOR]): counted in mechanisms and behaviours, not in lines; a change outside the scenario is scored ⚠️ "the user's choice" with what changes ([SCOPE-TRIGGER] in SKILL.md), never a ❌ by itself.
  7. **Risk:** the critics' findings against the core still open, each with its status and the case in the project that shows it ([RND-RISK-COST], [FIND-STATUS] in [findings.md](findings.md)), and every deviation from the standard ([RND-STANDARD]).
  8. **Shown:** the level at which the effect is shown ([FIND-LEVEL] in [findings.md](findings.md)), and what only the running product shows.

  A criterion of the "If applicable" list that applies gets a row of its own, with why it applies; maintainability over time is answered by rows 5–7 together with what it costs to change or remove later ([RND-RETURNS]). A cell is ✅ (holds: the report, file or value that shows it), ❌ (fails: what shows it) or ⚠️ (not shown either way: an unknown, never a low risk, [RND-EVIDENCE]; or open for the user's choice). A ❌ on row 1 or 2 is a core flaw ([RND-VERDICT]): the core isn't a candidate as it stands, [RND-NEXT] says whether it is revised, and one still ❌ at [RND-STOP] is out; a ⚠️ there leaves it a candidate with the risk named ([RND-DECIDING]). Among the candidates rows 3–7 rank (row 1 first: a ⚠️ there ranks below a ✅, [RND-EFFECT]), by what each ❌ or ⚠️ costs the user in the cell's words, not by the count of marks, and only where they tie, with equal uncertainty, the one that changes less ([RND-DECIDING]): a larger estimated effect is no tie; row 8 goes to the user's checklist. Where a row of 1–6 holds a ✅ on one core and a ❌ on another, candidate or not, and that ✅ is a mechanism the other core can carry, the judge names the merged core for the next round where it would close what neither closes alone ([RND-NEXT], [RND-VERDICT]). A trade on a kept item goes to the user as a choice; when nobody will answer, the choice among the candidates stands and the verdict and the report name the trade ([RND-ASSUMED]). The table stands in the judge's report, and its cells go into the trace as `attrs` on each variant's line, in one line with its `pro`, its `con` and `RND-BAR` in `r` ([RND-PROS]; `attrs` in [trace-agent.md](trace-agent.md)); the main thread's choice cites them. Challenge questions: which row did I not score, and what would it say? → score it before the verdict. Does a ⚠️ hide a ❌ the materials already show? → the ❌, with the material. Is "smaller" counted in lines where it should be counted in what else passes through the place? → row 6, by mechanisms.
  - Example: "a reminder goes out twice after a reschedule" → the core "drop the second job": ✅ effect, ❌ kept (a rescheduled booking loses its reminder: the scheduler's fact report), ✅ place; the core "an idempotency key where the reminder is sent": ✅ effect, ✅ kept, ✅ place, ✅ way, ⚠️ risk (a key collision across tenants: not shown, the critic found no such wiring), ⚠️ shown (not yet seen in the product) → the second is the candidate, the first is a core flaw to revise or drop, and the trade "no reminder after a reschedule" is named, not taken; etc.
  - Example: two candidates close every row but 4: one adds a cache the project doesn't have, the other answers from the project's existing index → row 4 ranks the second first; etc.
  - Anti-example: a table of scores with no evidence in the cells; "kept: partly" where a result stops in the scenario; a core ranked first for the smallest diff with its kept row unscored; the bar in the report but no `attrs` in the trace, so the user sees the winner and not why.

## Comparison and choice

- [RND-AUDIENCE] **The audience critic: the branches through the eyes of the product's user.** Where the task has a product's user, one more read-only critic ({{fresh}}) per round takes all the branches at once (in a later round, the ones that changed) and speaks as the project's target audience: who they are and what they expect comes from the profile's product section ([project-profile.md](project-profile.md)) and the user's words; with neither, the user the spec describes, marked as assumed. It reads the spec, each branch's result for the user and its evidence (the reproduction's recordings, pictures, reports), not the code, and answers for each branch, side by side, from the materials, with no run of the product: what I see and do now → after, in the spec's situations; a pre-mortem, what could go wrong for me; an imagined post-mortem, as if it had been in my hands a while, what I got against what the spec promised and what annoys, confuses or disappoints me (a jump, a wait, a missing response, an extra step, something that looks off, etc.); which one I'd rather have, and why: an input to the bar's cells for the user, not the recommendation ([RND-JUDGE]). What the request supplied as given (a named asset, an animation, a text, etc.) is the base it judges around: an observation or a question, not a complaint; the capture's own limits (its frame rate, the test stand, etc.) aren't the product's; a wish beyond the spec's items goes to the choice as an addition ([CHOICE-ITEMS] in SKILL.md), not into a score (the window stalls as it opens → scored; "I'd like it to remember the last tab" → an addition). A finding is a prediction with what it rests on, never "not checked". Its report feeds the bar's cells for the user ([RND-BAR]); a branch's revision gets only that branch's part of it ([RND-ISOLATE] in [rnd.md](rnd.md)).
  - Example: three ways to open a settings window → "the first snaps open with no transition and covers what I was doing; the second slides in, but its close button is far from where my hand is; the third keeps my place: I'd take the third"; etc.
  - Anti-example: the audience critic scores the code's complexity, or answers "the experience isn't verified yet".
- [RND-JUDGE] A separate judge sub-agent ({{fresh}}) scores every branch against the applicable criteria, in the bar's form ([RND-BAR]), using the critics' evidence. An unknown risk is never scored as low. Scoring is not delegated choosing: the main thread makes the choice.
  - [RND-PROS] **The judge writes what speaks for and against each branch into the trace: the conclusions of its scoring, not a list of their own.** A line with the branch's `id`, its `pro` and its `con` ([trace-agent.md](trace-agent.md)). The viewer shows them on each variant's card, so the user sees why the winner won without reading the critiques. Each point comes from a scored cell of the bar, for the user or technical, and says in a few words what the variant means for the player, the user or the project: what it fixes, what else it changes, etc. The point or points that decided the variant's place come first and say so ("decides: …", in the task's language); the rest follow by what they weigh, as many as the scoring gives. A rejected branch gets them too; what sank it is its deciding `con`. Challenge question: without this point, would the variant's place change, would the user choose differently, or is it a cost the user takes on with it? If none → drop it.
    - A con is what the variant costs or puts at risk, said in plain words like `u` ([TRACE-PLAIN]). A check still to do is not a con: it goes to the variant's `n`; its con, if any, is what goes wrong when the check fails.
    - Example: `{"id":"save-b","pro":["Decides: old saves keep loading after the update (one migration on load)","One place reads the format version"],"con":["The first load of a large old save takes a moment longer"]}`; etc.
    - Anti-examples: the same count on every card, as if filling a template, and the cell that decided the ranking in none of them; `"pro":["score 8"]` or `"con":["see critic 2"]` (a score or a pointer, not what it means); `"con":["compatibility with old saves has to be checked"]` (a check to do); the pros and cons only in the comparison table of the answer, so the variants on the graph show none.
- [RND-DECIDING] **A finding the choice hangs on gets checked; the rest are listed.** When a Real finding decides between branches (without it the winner would change), the tester checks it in a targeted Play Mode run before the choice: a Deep step ([PROFILE-STAGES]); in Fast and Normal it stays in the comparison: a limitation is closed or named at the choice ([RND-MENU] in [rnd.md](rnd.md)), and only a risk to what else the change touches heads QA. When such a run can't settle it, no branch is dropped on a finding checked no better than the winner's own risks: with equal uncertainty the variant that changes less wins, or the choice goes to the user.
  - Example: a critic reads that a narrow cache invalidation will thrash under load, which alone would drop it for a rewrite → a targeted load run decides; etc.
  - Anti-example: dropping the narrow branch on that reading while the rewrite's risks are just as unchecked.
- [RND-CHOICE] The main thread picks the winner and the runner-up, and states why the first idea won or lost.
